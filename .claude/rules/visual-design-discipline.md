## The frame

Claude is not asked to be a designer. Claude is asked to produce visual artifacts with specific behaviours that, applied consistently, make the result read as designed by a person rather than generated by a default. The behaviours are codified below as five fundamentals plus a vocabulary of AI tells to redirect. The behaviours override the LLM default visual register on every artifact.

The principle behind this rule is that good design and human-reading design are the same thing measured from two ends. AI-default design is the statistical median of the training distribution made visible. It is locally inoffensive at every axis and globally unmistakable when stacked. The cure is parametric: measurable deviation at every axis where defaults exist, with concrete values named and audit gates that count deviations. This is the visual counterpart of `.claude/rules/humanization.md` for prose. The same shape, a different medium.

Rule architecture: parametric leads, identity supplies the specifics. The mechanical fundamentals are constant. The concrete values (brand palette, typeface, spacing tokens) come from the company's brand authority. The brand authority is the newest deck under the registry name `brand-presentations` (its filename carries the revision date and is private, so it is not spelled here), in the private data overlay. The dashboard that served as its implementation left with the bridge daemon (HOS-7, 2026-09-21), and its markup is not recoverable from this repository's history. **This rule has no in-repo implementation to read.**

## Step 0 - Test before redesigning (calibration gate, mandatory)

Before applying the rewriting fundamentals below to an existing branded visual artifact, **test the baseline against the exemplar shelf**. Pull up three references: the artifact under review, the most analogous exemplar from the companion catalog, and one anti-pattern from the same reference. Hold them side by side. Three possible readings:

| Reading | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Artifact reads closer to the exemplar than to the anti-pattern | **Treat as known-good. Preserve byte-for-byte for the surfaces that already work.** Touch only what the explicit task requires. Apply restraint per `.claude/rules/development-standards.md`. A coherent visual register at this score is fragile to incremental edits in the same way detector-tested prose is fragile to punctuation normalisation. |
| Artifact reads halfway between exemplar and anti-pattern | **Diagnose the dominant defect first, then intervene specifically.** See Step 0a below. |
| Artifact reads closer to the anti-pattern than to the exemplar | **Content-first rewrite.** Run the five fundamentals in order, starting from specificity density (real metric names, real numbers, real customer/deal names). Only after that pass consider structural redesign. |

### Step 0a - Diagnose the dominant defect at borderline reading

At borderline readings, layout alone is not the answer. There are at least two distinct defect classes that the rule treats separately, and the right intervention depends on which one is dominant in the specific artifact:

**Defect A: AI-default surface.** Tailwind neutral stack, Inter or Geist alone, rounded-2xl on everything, purple-to-pink hero gradient, three-up feature cards, shadcn sidebar archetype. The artifact reads as scaffolded by v0, Lovable, bolt, Cursor, Gamma, Beautiful.ai, or similar. **When this is dominant, redirect aggressively.** Replace the typeface, replace the palette, break the centered-hero stack, kill the gradient, drop padding by 20-40%, switch from `rounded-2xl` to `rounded-md` or asymmetric radii (4/8/12px on the same view). Re-test.

**Defect B: Content-thin surface.** Layout is reasonable but copy is generic ("Our solution", "Build the future", "AI-powered"), labels are placeholders, numbers are rounded to vanity, charts use synthetic shapes. The artifact reads as a deck or dashboard for a generic company. **When this is dominant, do not redesign.** Replace every label with the actual operator-specific specific. £347,850 not £350,000. The actual customer name. The actual deal stage. The actual referrer. The actual issue ID. Re-test. If the layout still feels wrong, then redesign.

**How to diagnose.** Ask: does this artifact look like it could be from any company in the category, or does it look like your company specifically? If anonymous, Defect A. If specifically yours but reading as a generic version of your brand, Defect B. When in doubt, run the smaller intervention (one defect class) and re-test by holding the result against the exemplar shelf.

## The five fundamentals

Ordered by empirical strength. Specificity dominates. Structure is downstream. Apply in order.

### 1. Specificity density (the dominant signal)

**This is the most important fundamental.** Five research passes converged on one finding: the visual register that defeats AI-default detection is not a layout pattern, a palette, or a typeface. It is **real operator data populating real operator labels**. A dashboard that names the actual deal, the actual region, the actual partner is structurally indefeasible in a way that "Total Revenue / New Users / Churn Rate" with synthetic chart shapes is not.

**Every visual surface must contain at least one named, dated, or numbered specific drawn from real operator material.** Real customer names, real deal stages, real numeric values, real metric definitions in operator vocabulary, real timestamps, real referrers, real product strings (`ENG-2703`, `vehicle_state`). Not "Sarah M., Marketing Director." Not "Total Revenue." Not "approximately 50%."

Density benchmark from the Plausible reference dashboard (`plausible.io/plausible.io` is the load-bearing exemplar): every datum on the page is real, every label is the actual metric definition, the URLs in the Top Pages table are the actual top pages of the actual Plausible site. Roughly one named specific per cell of an information-dense view.

**Slide-level translation: the title is the assertion, not the label.** "Acme Platform classifies encrypted traffic at line rate without decryption" beats "Our Solution." "Three regional operators chose Acme in Q1 2026 after a proof of value" beats "Strong Demand." The slide title carries the argument; the slide body provides the proof.

**Fabricated specifics are worse than missing ones.** If the operator data is unavailable, ask. If it cannot be obtained in this pass, leave the cell empty or use a clearly bracketed placeholder (`[deal name TBD]`) rather than invent.

### 2. Committed stance - one opinionated view

Every artifact takes a position. Refuse to support every option equally. Linear commits to dark. Plausible commits to single-page-no-tabs. PostHog commits to hand-drawn hedgehogs in defiance of every blue-minimal analytics competitor. Mercury commits to commissioned editorial illustration and prints a bi-annual magazine. Notion's whole illustration system is hand-drawn by Roman Muradov.

Tabler's "light/dark parity treated as equal parents, not parent/child" is the signature anti-pattern. So is Salesforce's everything-everywhere. So is the Material 3 design system's tonal-palette extraction that produces a near-uniform primary blue across every implementation that does not customise.

**Concrete moves the rule enforces:**

- **One primary surface mode per artifact.** Dark or light. If both are required, declare one canonical and treat the other as derived, not co-equal.
- **One signature interaction or visual move per surface.** Linear's collapsible sidebar. Plausible's single-page layout. The brand's orange corner block (the deck design-system record in the operator's memory, in the data overlay). Refuse to multiply signatures.
- **Refused symmetry.** When the artifact admits a both-sides framing, take one side anyway and name it. The operator's voice rule already enforces this for prose; the visual rule extends it.

### 3. Hierarchy by intent (not uniform grid)

AI dashboards fail at hierarchy because RLHF rewards "balanced." Every metric card identical width, every chart identical height, every padding token the same. Real operator dashboards have **heterogeneous card weight**. The money metric occupies 60% or more of viewport above-fold. Everything else is supporting context. Datadog's density is legitimate because SRE specialists are trained on the chunks; Salesforce's density is illegitimate because every tile gets equal weight regardless of operator priority.

**Slide-level: the money slide.** One chart or number anchors the argument, gets full bleed, no decoration. Every other slide is supporting context. Stripe's keynote register does this aggressively: three data points centered on a slide, the speaker carries the rest, one Patrick Collison opener with no title at all because the audience already knows what they came to hear. <!-- content-guard: ok public figure cited as a design reference, not a workspace contact -->

**Parametric rules, all four human-judged.** They are written as numbers so two people reading the same artifact reach the same verdict, not because anything measures them: no engine computes a type-size ratio, a canvas-height fraction, a tile area, or an element count. Checking them is eyes on the artifact against the exemplar shelf.

- Heading-to-body type-size ratio ≥3.5x. If the headline is 56px, body cannot exceed 16px.
- Exactly one element per slide above 60% canvas height. Two equal-weight focal elements = no focal element.
- Bento tiles with equal sizes are a card layout in disguise. Mandate at least one tile at 2x the area of the smallest tile in any bento view.
- Above-fold cap: at most 4 discrete elements. The Pass 4 capture (Linear, Vercel, Stripe, Resend, Plausible) is the shelf this bound was read off, but it recorded screenshots and no element counts, so treat it as the reference set to compare against rather than as a measurement that proves the bound.

### 4. Restricted palette, signature accent

**One brand base. One strong accent. Three to five greys tinted toward the brand hue.** No two saturated colors at hero density. No gradient hero text. No purple-to-pink hero gradient (the single most-cited tell across every source - Anthropic's own cookbook bans it as the "AI slop signature").

**Parametric rule.** Two of these four are mechanised; two are not, and the difference is marked on each.

- **(human-judged)** Delta-E ≥12 between non-adjacent elements (Grok's measurable contrast rule from the council pass). It reads as a threshold and is written as one, but nothing computes Delta-E: `grep -ci "delta.e" scripts/visual-discipline-check.py scripts/utils/impeccable_engine.py` returns 0 in both engines. Judge it by eye, or measure it by hand.
- **(human-judged)** One saturated accent per surface. If the brand has two (a brand orange + a product blue, per the deck design-system record in the operator's memory), one leads per surface; the other is supporting. No engine counts accents.
- **(mechanised, error)** Gradient backgrounds are permitted only when the gradient is a commissioned brand asset, not a CSS template. Stripe's purple-magenta-coral ribbon (commissioned) is permitted in spirit. Vercel's brand-orange radial (commissioned) is permitted in spirit. `bg-gradient-to-r from-purple-500 to-pink-500` (Tailwind default) is forbidden. The regex engine catches the Tailwind and CSS forms; whether a gradient is genuinely commissioned it cannot know.
- **(mechanised, error)** Pastel violet-pink-lavender at hero density is forbidden. Material 3's `~#E8DDF4`, Gamma's `~#D0E4F5`, Tabler's `~#F5EEF8` are the captured tells. The regex engine matches those three literal hexes and legacy ChatGPT emerald `#10A37F`; "at hero density" is the human half of the judgement, since a hex match says nothing about where the colour sits.

### 5. Typography chosen with intent

Anthropic's frontend cookbook explicitly bans Inter, Roboto, Open Sans, Lato, and default system fonts. This workspace already commits to GT Standard for documents; the commitment is written down in the corporate style guide, which now lives in the data repository at `$(python scripts/data-path.py path corporate-style-guide)`, and the font files and their family-name mapping are in the data overlay under `fonts/` of the registry name `brand-kit` (the record on Pencil deck fonts and the master template, in the operator's memory). The new rule extends commitment to interface design.

**Permitted as primary:**

- **GT Standard family** for all corporate documents (proposals, letters, partnership docs, official docs, xPagers, briefing HTML/PDF). This is locked per the corporate-docs guardrail and the corporate style guide.
- **Geist family** (Geist Sans, Geist Mono, Geist Pixel) for public-web surfaces where Vercel's commissioned register resonates. Geist is in 2026 the new statistical median for developer-aimed sites; use with awareness, prefer a custom variant where overlap with Vercel identity becomes a tell.
- **IBM Plex family** (Sans, Mono, Serif) as the open-source fallback when GT Standard is unavailable in the target system.
- **Custom commissions** for high-stakes surfaces where the typographic signature is part of the brand argument.

**Forbidden as primary:**

- Inter (Pass 1 cited: "growing ubiquity in the SaaS industry means using it may make your product look like every other SaaS product").
- Roboto, Open Sans, Lato, Poppins, Montserrat (the budget-tier defaults).
- Default system fonts when the artifact carries company identity.

The forbidden-as-primary list above is the one part of this fundamental a machine enforces: the regex engine matches those seven family names inside `font-family`, a Google Fonts `family=` URL, an OOXML `typeface=` attribute, or a CSS font variable, and reports each as an error.

**Permitted patterns. All four are human-judged; no engine measures weight, size ratio, tracking, or typeface count.**

- Weight extremes per Anthropic: 100/200 vs 800/900, not 400 vs 600.
- Size jumps of 3x or more between hierarchy levels, not 1.5x.
- Negative tracking on display (Linear's `-0.22px` at 56px is the reference).
- Maximum two typefaces per artifact, ideally one. Mixing sans and serif on the same slide requires a defensible reason.

## The vocabulary of AI tells

Catalog of patterns the rule names for redirection. Full per-tell expansion lives in the companion catalog, `visual-design-discipline.md` under `reference-docs`. The summary below is the audit checklist.

| Category | Tells to forbid or redirect |
|---|---|
| Color | Purple-to-pink hero gradient, indigo-violet primary accents (Tailwind defaults), legacy ChatGPT emerald `#10A37F` as primary, pastel rainbow gradient blobs, the Tailwind neutral stack (`slate-50` + `zinc-900`) without modification |
| Typography | Inter / Roboto / Open Sans / Lato / Poppins / Montserrat as primary, Space Grotesk as the "I tried" upgrade, JetBrains Mono code overlays on non-devtool products, mid-weight monoculture (font-medium headings + font-normal body) |
| Layout | Centered-hero stack (eyebrow + 64pt headline + subhead + two CTAs), three-up icon-and-description feature cards in `rounded-2xl`, the shadcn dashboard archetype (sidebar-left, table-center, button-group-top-right), equal-size bento tiles, "Trusted by" logo strip immediately under the hero, FAQ accordion + three-column footer, big empty hero with no message |
| Components | The shadcn card composition (Avatar + Title + Description + optional Action), Lucide outline icons at 24px / 1.5px stroke / rounded caps as default, Heroicons outline as the Tailwind-house alternative, pricing toggle + three-tier cards with "Recommended" outline |
| Motion | Framer Motion `type: "spring"` with default `bounce`, stagger-reveal on page load, hover-scale-1.02 on cards, universal 200-300ms ease-out transitions |
| Assets | AI-generated decorative illustrations tonally matched to the palette (the Gamma giveaway), the isometric 3D illustration return, default Unsplash stock with shallow DOF and cool grading |
| Copy register | "Build the future" / "AI-powered" / "Reimagine X" taglines, "Ship X. Build Y." imperative pairs, generic CTAs ("Get started", "Learn more"), em-dash overuse in feature copy, Title Case For Every Heading |
| Dashboard composite | shadcn sidebar + 4-stat-card row with Lucide icons and "vs last month" deltas + Recharts area chart card + data table with avatar+name+badge+amount rows + `+ New` button in top-right primary accent (v0 "Dashboard 01" template) |
| Presentation composite | Vertical card layout (Gamma's signature), purple-to-blue background, Inter alone on every slide, three-card feature row, generic titles ("Our solution", "Why us"), hallucinated testimonials ("Sarah M., Marketing Director"), Title Case headings, brand voice not carrying through visually |
| Enterprise refusal | Site behind Akamai bot detection (Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow refuse headless visitors). A vendor that mimics this defensive funnel posture inherits the defensive register |

The single structural pattern that is always banned: **Title Case For All Headings**. Use sentence case. Title Case is print-magazine convention that RLHF picked up from training data.

### Three reflexes no detector catches

Added 2026-08-09 from the upstream craft floor (`pbakaus/impeccable`, Apache 2.0). Each is a thing the generated version of a page reliably omits, and none of them is mechanically checkable.

- **Theme the browser surfaces you did not draw.** Text selection, the caret, the focus ring, custom scrollbars, underline offset, and the numerals in tabular data all ship with browser defaults belonging to no design system. Theming them from the palette is the cheapest signal that a page was built rather than assembled, and the one models skip most reliably.
- **Declare elevation once: a border or a shadow, never both.** A hairline border under a wide soft shadow is the ghost card, a recurring generated signature. Pick the defined edge or the soft lift and commit to it.
- **Icons are drawn, never typed.** Unicode glyphs and emoji standing in for an icon system are a tell. Use a real icon library or authored SVG, at one consistent stroke and weight.

## The asset rule

Stock people-photography is banned. This includes generic offices, hands at keyboards, lightbulbs, gears, three-people-pointing-at-a-laptop, diverse teams in branded T-shirts, founders-in-suits portraiture, customer-headshots-on-coloured-circles.

**Custom-generated illustration is permitted only under one of two constraints:**

- **2-bit monochrome constraint.** Two values, one accent. Line art at line weight matched to the artifact's body type. Examples in the exemplar shelf: Stripe Press editorial illustrations, Notion's Roman Muradov ink work, basic team maritime metaphor illustrations.
- **Commissioned-style register.** The illustration must read as commissioned editorial (Mercury), commissioned animation (Active Theory), or commissioned mascot (PostHog's hedgehog). AI-generated illustrations that tonally match the slide palette are forbidden because they read as the Gamma signature.

Real product screenshots are always permitted. Real team photography is always permitted. Real customer-installation photography is always permitted when the customer has cleared distribution.

## The carve-out: when defaults are permitted

This rule is register-conditional. Internal admin and utility surfaces are exempt because the AI-default register on those surfaces is not register-conditional brand surface. Tabler, AdminLTE, or default Material 3 on an internal-only ops dashboard nobody sees outside the team carries no identity penalty.

**In scope (full rule applies):**

- Investor materials, board materials, fundraising decks, data room artifacts.
- Customer-facing product UI (an analyst dashboard, AI assistant surfaces, a customer console).
- Marketing site, product pages, status pages.
- Briefing HTML and PDF (intel-briefing-newsletter, market-brief, ceo-intel, dashboard, weekly-review outputs).
- Decks: keynote-deck, pptx-generator outputs, investor-pitch outputs, proposal renders.
- Corporate documents per the five locked doctypes (corporate-letter, proposal, partnership-doc, official-doc, xpager).

**Out of scope (rule does not apply):**

- Internal ops scripts, daemon logs, machine output.
- Code, JSON, structured machine data viz consumed by Claude or by automation.
- Throwaway HTML reports for personal CEO use (the registry name `operations-scratch`, anything in the data root's runtime store).
- Third-party content displayed verbatim (citations, quoted material, brand assets from counterparties shown for reference).

## Validation requirement

Every visual deliverable presented to the operator must include the standard confirmation line:

> Visual register: [register name]. AI tells found: clean / N findings (and a one-line summary of redirects applied if any). Specificity density: X named specifics per artifact.

The mechanical tells are audited by `scripts/visual-discipline-check.py`, which runs **two engines**, and the difference between them decides what a clean result is worth.

**The regex engine** (default, always runs, shipped 2026-06-26) scans HTML/SVG/PPTX for forbidden fonts, the purple->pink gradient, oversized Tailwind radii (`rounded-2xl`/`3xl`), Lucide/Heroicons defaults, and the banned hero colors (ChatGPT emerald, captured pastels) as errors, plus advisory layout/copy heuristics (neutral-stack pairing, indigo-violet primary, three-up cards, centered hero, Title Case headings). It sees what is WRITTEN DOWN.

**The deep engine** (`--deep`, added 2026-08-09) is the impeccable CLI (`pbakaus/impeccable`, Apache 2.0, pinned in `scripts/.impeccable-version`). It parses the HTML, resolves the CSS cascade, and computes real values: text contrast against the surface actually behind it, heading hierarchy, accent stripes on rounded corners, type floors, kickers, buzzword and cadence tells in copy. It sees what RENDERS. Deep findings carry an `impeccable:` type prefix so a reader can tell, per finding, which engine made the claim. It reads HTML and SVG only.

**What is actually scanned, and what is not.** The regex walk visits exactly the suffixes in `SCAN_EXTENSIONS` in `scripts/visual-discipline-check.py`, which is `.html`, `.htm`, `.svg`, `.pptx`. The deep engine adds nothing to that set; it narrows it to HTML and SVG. So:

| Format | Regex engine | Deep engine |
|---|---|---|
| HTML / HTM | yes | yes |
| SVG | yes | yes |
| PPTX | yes (OOXML parts unzipped: fonts and colors only) | no |
| **PDF** | **no** | **no** |
| **DOCX** | **no** | **no** |
| PNG and every other raster | no | no |

**PDF and DOCX are read by nothing.** This rule used to say they "stay regex-only", which implied a scan that has never existed for either. That matters most where the rule is strictest: `## The carve-out` below puts all five locked corporate doctypes in scope, and every one of them renders to PDF and four of them to DOCX. Those renders are audited by no engine at all, and the only check on them is a person looking at the page.

PNG is in this rule's `paths:` globs and in no engine either. The globs decide when the rule loads for whoever is working; they are not a claim that anything scans the file.

```bash
python scripts/visual-discipline-check.py <file-or-dir>              # regex only
python scripts/visual-discipline-check.py --deep <file-or-dir>       # both engines
python scripts/visual-discipline-check.py --deep --profile doctype <file>
python scripts/visual-discipline-check.py baseline check --deep docs/
```

Three things sit between the deep engine and a finding anyone acts on. The first two are declared in `config/visual-check-profiles.json`; the third is a separate file, and calling all three config-declared was wrong.

- **Profiles** (`config/visual-check-profiles.json`). An A4 document is judged by print rules and a screen surface by screen rules; the 11px type floor is a screen floor and says nothing true about a printed page. Where a locked corporate template and a detector rule disagree, **the template wins** - the xPager kicker is approved brand, and `.claude/rules/corporate-docs.md` puts changes to it behind CEO approval.
- **Plausibility bounds** (`config/visual-check-profiles.json`). The parser emits impossible readings on some of our CSS (an h1 at 2856px, a line-height of 0.11x). Those are filtered on VALUE, never by disabling the rule, so genuine hits on the same rule still land.
- **The baseline** (`.visual-baseline.json`, at the repo root, not in the profiles config). It freezes what existed on 2026-08-09. The gate fires on findings ABOVE that line. Existing artifacts are recorded, not remediated; the real defects that were found and left unfixed are listed in the calibration report under the registry name `design-check-reports`. **Do not quote the size of that freeze from memory** - a number typed into prose rots the moment anyone re-records. Derive it:

  ```bash
  python scripts/visual-discipline-check.py baseline stats
  ```

Two honest limits on a clean deep result. An **unused CSS rule is not a finding** - the cascade is resolved against real elements, so dead styles are invisible. And `npx --yes` **fetches and executes third-party code** at call time; the exact version pin is the only mitigation claimed, matching what the workspace already accepts for marp-cli, and it is weaker than a hash-verified install.

### What is mechanised, and what is a person reading

Neither engine judges the first three fundamentals - specificity density, committed stance, hierarchy by intent. Those are human, against the exemplar shelf. Fundamentals 4 and 5 are **partly** mechanised, and this rule used to imply they were fully so by scoping the human-judged carve-out to "the first three".

Everything an engine reports at **error** severity comes from four sources, and that is the whole mechanised set:

| Mechanised check | Where it is defined |
|---|---|
| Forbidden primary fonts (7 families) | `FORBIDDEN_FONTS` in `scripts/visual-discipline-check.py` |
| Banned hero hexes (4) | `BANNED_COLORS` in the same file |
| The purple-to-pink gradient, Tailwind and CSS forms | `_GRAD_FROM` / `_GRAD_TO` / `_CSS_GRADIENT` in the same file |
| Rendered contrast, heading hierarchy, type floors, kickers, cadence | the impeccable CLI, on HTML and SVG only |

Everything else this rule states as a number - Delta-E ≥12, the 3.5x type ratio, the 60% canvas share, the 2x bento tile, the 4-element above-fold cap, negative tracking, the two-typeface ceiling, "one saturated accent", "at hero density" - is **human-judged**. Each is marked as such where it appears above.

### What enforces this rule

One thing, and it is narrower than the rule used to claim.

**Nothing enforces it.** No CI step runs `scripts/visual-discipline-check.py`, so nothing in this repo fails on a visual finding.

**No skill runs the audit** (an earlier claim that fifteen did was never true). Verify in one command:

```bash
grep -rl "visual-discipline-check" .claude/skills/    # no matches
```

The skill-side step is therefore an **unenforced convention**: a skill that renders an HTML or SVG artifact SHOULD run the checker on it before declaring done, and if it does not, nothing notices. Wiring it in was considered and deliberately not done in one pass, because for the four locked-doctype skills whose only renders are PDF and DOCX the checker would read nothing and return clean, which replaces a missing check with a false one. Wiring the audit into a skill is a change to workspace infrastructure and needs the operator's approval; the honest state until then is written here rather than papered over.

No live test holds this rule and the skills in step since 2026-09-21; whoever changes either side is the one who checks.

The CEO can request the audit at any time during a session.

## Failure modes to watch

- **Over-scrubbing without replacing (the vacuum trap).** Banned-pattern removal that leaves nothing in its place produces empty, lifeless artifacts. Removal must be paired with replacement: kill the gradient, name the brand voltage that replaces it.
- **Mechanical 1-spread-1-tight parametric rhythm.** Mechanical compliance with the deviation count (every artifact has exactly 4 deviations on a "human offset" layer) is itself a pattern an audit will eventually recognize. The rule mandates *deliberate variance*, not a metronome of variance.
- **Forced specifics that are fabricated.** "Acme Platform saved Customer X 17 days of incident response time" is humanising only if it is also true. Inventing specifics is worse than generalising. When the specifics are unknown, ask the operator or leave a bracketed placeholder.
- **Identity-level mimicry without substrate.** "Use the company brand voice" applied without consulting the Product Deck produces caricature, not voice.
- **Ignoring the rule on "internal" output that is in fact external.** An "internal" dashboard that becomes a screenshot in a press piece was not internal. When in doubt about scope, apply the rule.

## When the rule cannot apply

Four exceptions and only four:

1. **Internal admin and utility surfaces** per the carve-out above.
2. **Code, configuration, structured data viz** consumed by Claude, by daemons, or by automation. Not user-facing visual artifacts.
3. **Third-party content displayed verbatim.** Citations, quoted reports, counterparty brand assets shown for reference.
4. **CEO-supplied draft Claude is rendering.** When Claude is the producer, the rule applies. When Claude is rendering a CEO-supplied PSD, sketch, or hand-marked annotation, follow the CEO's choices and surface concerns only on confirmation.

## Change control

Updates to this rule require the operator's explicit approval. The vocabulary of AI tells is expected to drift on a 6 to 9 month cadence as the dominant generators update their defaults and the design discourse identifies new tells. The Last Verified date triggers a re-audit when older than 9 months.
